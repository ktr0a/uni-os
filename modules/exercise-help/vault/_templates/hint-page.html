1. The technique on a different example

Claim: $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (2i - 1) = n^2$ for every $n \ge 1$ template sample

Base case $n = 1$: the left side is $2 \cdot 1 - 1 = 1$ and the right side is $1^2 = 1$.

Induction step. Assume $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (2i - 1) = n^2$ for some $n \ge 1$ (induction hypothesis). Then

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} (2i - 1) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (2i - 1) + (2(n+1) - 1) = n^2 + 2n + 1 = (n+1)^2.$$

The first equality splits off the last term, the second uses the hypothesis, the third is the binomial formula.

Where this usually goes wrong: splitting off the wrong term (the last term of the sum up to $n+1$ is the one with $i = n + 1$), and using the claim for $n + 1$ instead of proving it.

2. Your task: hint ladder

Open one rung, try again for a while, then open the next if you are still stuck.

Hint 1: which technique applies

The claim is about every natural number $n \ge 1$ and contains a sum up to $n$: induction on $n$ fits, exactly as in section 1.

Hint 2: the first step

Check the base case with the smallest $n$ the claim talks about, and write the induction hypothesis down word for word before you use it.

Hint 3: the intermediate claim to aim for

In the step, after splitting off the last term and using the hypothesis, aim for an expression you can recognise as the right-hand side with $n + 1$ in place of $n$.

Top rung: the missing idea in one line (only after the time cap)

Write the sum up to $n+1$ as the sum up to $n$ plus its last term.

3. Check your own steps

  • Does your base case use the smallest $n$ in the claim, and do both sides agree?
  • Is the induction hypothesis stated for a fixed $n$, and used only once you have split the sum?
  • Did you end with exactly the claim for $n + 1$ (not just "this looks right")?
  • Test the claim on $n = 1, 2, 3$ by hand before you trust your algebra.

What a finished answer looks like: base case (two lines), the hypothesis (one line), the step (a chain of equalities, one reason per equality), a closing sentence that names the principle of induction.
